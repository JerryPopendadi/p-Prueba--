There are two ways to invest in a spa: buy into one that already works, or join a project before it opens. Each has its own balance of risk, timing and potential. Here is how to compare them, using the four THE FACES projects in Bali as real examples.

Operating spa: see it before you invest

An operating spa has guests, a team and a history. You can review months of accounts, check booking data and book a treatment yourself.

  • Pros: real figures to verify, revenue from day one, a team already trained.
  • Cons: the value already reflects the work done, so the entry price is based on a proven business.
  • Example: THE FACES Ubud, operating for about three years · $300,000 · up to 49% of shares and up to 50% of profit.

New project: enter before the launch

A pre-opening project needs capital to finish construction, buy equipment and launch. A partner who joins at this stage helps shape the opening.

  • Pros: entry before the business is proven, a new concept, a fresh fit-out.
  • Cons: construction delays, slower ramp-up or lower demand are possible.
  • Examples: The White Temple, Sanur, renovation under way, 10-year lease signed · $250,000; WATERFALL, Ubud, under construction · $300,000; THE FACES SANUR, plans ready, 250 m² opposite the Hyatt · $450,000, 50/50.

How new projects reduce risk

  • Signed leases: 10 years for The White Temple and THE FACES SANUR (partly prepaid).
  • Capital in tranches: for WATERFALL, funds are proposed to be released against construction and launch milestones from a dedicated project account.
  • An experienced operator: the same team that runs THE FACES spas in Bali runs every new project.
