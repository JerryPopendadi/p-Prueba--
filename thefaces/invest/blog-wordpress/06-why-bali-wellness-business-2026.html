Bali has always been a place people visit to feel better. Long before the word "wellness" became an industry, the island was known for its temples, its healing traditions and its slow, ritual rhythm of life. Today that culture meets a fast-growing global market, and that is why more private partners are looking at Bali spas.

1. Visitors keep coming back

Bali welcomed 6.95 million foreign visitors in 2025, 9.7% more than in 2024 (BPS-Statistics Indonesia, Bali Province). Many of them plan their trip around wellness: massages, rituals, yoga mornings and quiet afternoons in a spa. For a well-located spa, that means a steady flow of guests throughout the year.

2. Wellness is a global economy, not a niche

The Global Wellness Institute values the global wellness economy at US$6.8 trillion in 2024, with a forecast close to US$9.8 trillion by 2029. The global spa sector alone was US$137 billion in 2023, projected to reach US$184 billion by 2028. These figures describe the market, not the results of any single business, but they show that people around the world are spending more on feeling well.

3. Ritual is part of the island's identity

Offerings on temple steps, the sound of water in rice terraces, ceremonies at sunset over the cliffs of Uluwatu: in Bali, ritual is everyday life. A spa concept that respects this culture feels authentic to guests, and authenticity is what makes people return and recommend.

4. Two very different markets in one island

  • Ubud: jungle, rice terraces and wellness travellers who stay several nights and look for signature rituals.
  • Sanur: a calm beach town with hotels, couples and returning visitors, where street visibility brings guests in.

Read our comparison: Ubud vs Sanur: where to own a spa in Bali.

5. What to check before you become a partner

  1. The operator. Who runs the spa every day, and for how long have they done it?
  2. The lease. Duration, prepaid period and transfer clauses.
  3. The numbers. Booking data and bank statements, not headline percentages.
  4. The structure. How your shareholding is registered and how you can exit.
