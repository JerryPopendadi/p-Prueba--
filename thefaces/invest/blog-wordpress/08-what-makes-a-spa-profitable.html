A beautiful spa is not automatically a profitable one. Behind the candles and the music, every spa runs on a handful of numbers. If you understand these five, you can read any spa's accounts and ask the right questions.

1. Utilisation

The share of available treatment hours that are actually booked. It is the single biggest lever on profit: rent and core salaries are paid whether rooms are full or empty, so every extra booked hour adds directly to the result.

Ask for: booked hours ÷ available hours, month by month, for at least a year.

2. Average ticket

Revenue per visit. Signature rituals, longer programmes and couples treatments raise it; quick, discounted services lower it. In THE FACES SANUR project, both floors are planned around an average ticket of about IDR 1 million.

Ask for: revenue ÷ number of visits, and the treatment mix.

3. Repeat guests

New guests cost money to attract. Returning guests and prepaid packages bring revenue at almost no marketing cost. A strong concept and consistent service are what bring people back.

Ask for: share of returning guests and packages sold.

4. Cost base

Rent, team, products, utilities and management fees. Fixed costs must be covered before any profit appears. A long, partly prepaid lease makes the biggest fixed cost predictable.

Ask for: fixed costs per month and variable costs as a percentage of revenue.

5. Break-even occupancy

The utilisation needed to cover every cost. The lower it is, the more resilient the spa is in a quiet month.

Ask for: the actual break-even figure for each location.

FAQ

What is a good utilisation for a spa?

It depends on the location, the cost base and the opening hours. The right question is how far utilisation is above break-even, month after month.

What drives a high average ticket?

Signature rituals, longer programmes, couples treatments and a premium setting.

Are spa profits stable?

They vary with seasons and demand. Always review at least twelve months of data.
