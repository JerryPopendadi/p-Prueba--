Headline percentages are easy to print and hard to verify. Serious investors look at booking data, bank statements and contracts. Whether you are considering a spa in Bali or anywhere else, these are the 12 documents to request before you invest, and what each one tells you.

Financial documents

  1. Management accounts, month by month. Revenue, costs and profit for at least 12 months, to see the seasons.
  2. Bank statements. They confirm that the revenue in the accounts actually reached the bank.
  3. Booking-system sales data. Number of guests, treatments sold and average ticket, straight from the booking software.
  4. Tax filings. What was declared, and that the company is up to date.

Operating documents

  1. Treatment menu and prices. Is the average ticket realistic for the area?
  2. Staff structure and payroll. The largest cost in most spas.
  3. Utilisation. The share of available treatment hours actually booked: the single biggest lever on profit.
  4. Break-even occupancy. The utilisation needed to cover all costs. The lower it is, the more resilient the business.

Legal documents

  1. Lease agreement. Duration, prepaid period, renewal and transfer.
  2. Licences and permits. Business registration and the permits for spa activity.
  3. Shareholder agreement. Profit share, reporting, veto rights, exit, transfer and pre-emption.
  4. Management agreement. What the operator does, its fees and how performance is reported.

Five questions to ask the founders

  • What happens to my share if the spa has a weak month?
  • Who decides on new debt, a new lease or a budget change?
  • How and when is the profit paid?
  • Can I visit the spa and meet the team?
  • How can I exit, and on what terms?

FAQ

How long does spa due diligence take?

Usually a few weeks, depending on how quickly documents are shared and reviewed by your advisers.

Should I visit in person?

If possible, yes. If not, ask for a live video walkthrough of the premises and a call with the team.

What is a red flag?

Figures that cannot be matched to bank statements or booking data, a short or unclear lease, and no written exit terms.
